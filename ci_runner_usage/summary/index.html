Spyre CI usage: executive summary

Nov 4, 2026 · Spyre self-hosted runner-hours, Oct 19 – Nov 1 (14 days), across spyre-inference, torch-spyre and hf-adapters.

spyre-inference used the biggest slice of shared Spyre CI time: 46%, about 16k of 34.6k runner-hours in two weeks. Two-thirds of that came from integration runs that torch-spyre and other repos dispatch to us (spyre-test). Five fixes have merged since Oct 23, and three more PRs are in flight. Together they roughly halve spyre-inference's usage and cut the three repos' total by about a fifth.

How the "after" numbers are estimated
  • "Today" is the main report's §2 measurement. The spyre-test bar is split using the compile-cache census of all 767 dispatched runs: 30.5% was jobs left running after a cancel (#1353), 49.4% integration-tier and 19.4% regression-tier work. Those shares are scaled to the report's 10,327 h.
  • #1499 and #1501 scale those integration and regression hours by the per-run costs on the axis 3 page.
  • #1539's estimate is the case's warm-run cost (7.4 min, from the JUnit of a warm PR run) times about 317 full test_each_commit runs per 14 days. The case was never in the integration tier, so dispatched runs don't change.
  • The axis 1 and 2 PRs are counted only on our own CI. There the cache is warm, so they save little: about 3% of test_each_commit. On cold dispatched runs they save more, but those minutes overlap with what #1499 already removes, so they aren't added again. #1501 also moves our own PRs to trunk, which leaves our own CI's cost unchanged.
  • torch-spyre and hf-adapters are held flat. Their figures come from 12–40-run samples and could be off by up to 2×, so their bars are approximate.
